五、Verification methods, just follow them

For certificates, take the number to the National Medical Products Administration's "Cosmetics Production License Information" or the provincial bureau platform to check, and verify the three items — company name, address, and licensed items; only when all three match does it count.

The official verification entry point is nmpa.gov.cn: go to "Cosmetics Production License Information" and search by company name, comparing item by item the name, production address, and licensed items; for filing information, check by filing number in the National Medical Products Administration's "Cosmetics Filing Information."

For filings, ask the factory for the filing number of the category you are preparing to make, and check the ingredients and efficacy claims yourself; do not just look at the screenshot provided by the factory.

For a factory audit, look at only three places: the differential pressure gauge in the clean area of the filling line, the batch labels in the raw material warehouse, and the retention sample records in the retention sample room. These three places cannot be faked; everything else can be staged. For capacity, do not look at the PPT; look at the current month's production schedule and work orders in progress.

六、Clarification of common misunderstandings

There is a saying online that "for small OEM/ODM factories, getting a certificate is just a piece of paper." Upon verification, this mostly originates from a group of early cases of small workshops that only did filling and did not do filings, and is inconsistent with the current situation. The way to judge now is simple: take the license number to the provincial bureau platform and check; if the name, address, and licensed items can be found, it is a valid certificate; if it cannot be found, nothing else matters.

There is also the saying that "low MOQ necessarily means poor quality." This comes from the early stage when small batches could only be filled manually. In fact, MOQ and quality have no causal relationship; what determines quality is the workshop grade, the retention sample system, and the testing frequency — these three items. Small batches can likewise run on fully automatic lines; it is just that the packaging material MOQ will raise the per-unit cost.

There is also the saying that "a low quote means low total cost." The total cost is the sum of the number of sampling rounds, the rework rate, and packaging material loss; the line on the quotation sheet is only the beginning. For the same foundation liquid, a 15% difference in quotes is common, but the cost of one rework usually exceeds that difference.
