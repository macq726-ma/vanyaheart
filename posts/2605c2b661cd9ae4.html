Why the Grade of a Cosmetic Raw Material Matters More Than Its Name

This article answers: why does the grade of a cosmetic raw material matter more than its name?

Formulation and process problems rarely have a single isolated cause. Below, we break it down in the order of "why this happens — which parameter it gets stuck on — how to verify."

一、Direct Answer

In one sentence: when this phenomenon occurs in this type of product, the main cause is usually not any single raw material, but the earlier part of the causal chain. Raw materials under the same INCI name may come from different process routes: synthetic and natural sources differ in impurity profile, particle size, and moisture content. This is the practical meaning of "grade matters more than name" — changing the grade is equivalent to changing a variable. The solution is not to switch to a more expensive raw material, but to fix the few parameters on this chain that truly determine the outcome into a window, then use retained batch samples to confirm that every batch falls within the window.

二、Mechanism: Why This Happens

The underlying logic of this step is to stabilize two incompatible phases together.

First, raw materials under the same INCI name may come from different process routes: synthetic and natural sources differ in impurity profile, particle size, and moisture content. This is the practical meaning of "grade matters more than name" — changing the grade is equivalent to changing a variable.

Second, a raw material COA must be checked for at least four items: batch number and production date, measured values of key indicators (not the specification range), test method number, and issuing party; a COA that only has a specification range without measured values is equivalent to providing no batch information.

On parameters, the industry already has clear acceptance criteria; there is no need to rely on experience and guesswork.

The cosmetic safety assessment report is issued by assessors with the appropriate competence, and its content must cover three parts: raw material toxicology, exposure amount, and risk characterization; the report is not an attachment to the filing, but one of the substantive conditions for filing.

For export to the EU, what needs to be prepared is the CPSR (Cosmetic Product Safety Report), the PIF (Product Information File), and the CPNP notification number; the MSDS and COA are documents at the transport and batch level, and are not the same set of things as compliance market access.

The credibility of a test report depends on three points: whether the issuing institution holds the appropriate accreditation, whether the report number can be verified on the institution's official website, and whether the sample source and batch number match the received batch; if any one of these is missing, the report cannot serve as a basis for acceptance.

四、Common Misconceptions

There are several widely circulated claims that do not hold up under actual testing.

The belief that natural raw materials are necessarily milder than synthetic ones — naturally sourced ingredients have more complex compositions and greater batch-to-batch variation, and the sensitization risk is often higher; whether something is mild depends on purity and the formulation system, not on the source label.

五、How to Verify

This section is an actionable checklist; any item can be requested on the spot.

Step one: determine whether the raw material has been swapped — compare the batch number on the COA with the three key indicators (content, particle size or viscosity, impurities); physical properties of different batches of the same grade are allowed to fluctuate, but the fluctuation range should fall within the range declared by the supplier.

Step two: determine whether the raw material has had a grade change — compare the key indicators on this batch's COA side by side with the previous batch: if any one of the three items — content, viscosity or particle size, impurities — exceeds the historical fluctuation range, the formula must be re-validated as a new raw material.

Step three: troubleshoot color difference in a fixed order — first compare the retained sample of the same batch with historical retained samples, then check whether the raw material batch was changed, then check the grinding and dispersion parameters, and only lastly check the standing time before filling; reversing the order will keep you circling in the wrong step.
