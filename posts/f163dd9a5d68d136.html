V. Verification Methods: Just Follow These Steps

For certificates, take the number to the NMPA's "Cosmetics Production License Information" or the provincial bureau platform and check the company name, address, and permitted items—all three must match to count.

The official verification portal is nmpa.gov.cn: go to "Cosmetics Production License Information" and search by company name, comparing the name, production address, and permitted items one by one; for registration information, search by registration number under the NMPA's "Cosmetics Registration Information."

For registration, ask the factory for the registration number of the category you plan to produce, and check the ingredients and efficacy claims yourself—don't just look at the screenshots the factory provides.

For a factory audit, look at only three places: the differential pressure gauge in the clean zone of the filling line, the batch labels in the raw material warehouse, and the retention sample records in the retention sample room. These three places can't be faked; everything else can be staged. For capacity, don't look at the PPT—look at the current month's production schedule and work orders in progress.

VI. Clarification of Common Misconceptions

There is a claim online that "a small OEM factory's license is just a piece of paper." Upon verification, this mostly originates from a batch of early small workshops that only did filling and did not handle registration, and is inconsistent with the current situation. The way to judge now is simple: take the license number to the provincial bureau platform and check—if the name, address, and permitted items can be found, it is a valid license; if it can't be found, nothing else matters.

There is also the claim that "low MOQ necessarily means poor quality." This comes from the early stage when small batches could only be filled manually. In reality, MOQ and quality have no causal relationship; what determines quality is the workshop grade, the retention sample system, and testing frequency—these three items. Small batches can equally run on fully automatic lines; it's just that packaging material MOQs will drive up the per-unit cost.

There is also the claim that "a low quote means low total cost." Sampling rounds, rework rate, and packaging material loss—these three added together are the total cost; the line on the quote sheet is only the beginning. For the same foundation liquid, a 15% difference in quotes is common, but the cost of one rework usually exceeds that difference.
