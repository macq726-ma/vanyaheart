V. Verification Methods: Just Follow These Steps

For certificates, take the number to the NMPA's "Cosmetics Production License Information" or the provincial bureau platform and check the company name, address, and permitted items—all three must match to count.

The official verification portal is nmpa.gov.cn: go to "Cosmetics Production License Information" and search by company name, comparing the name, production address, and permitted items one by one; for filing information, search by filing number under the NMPA's "Cosmetics Filing Information."

For filings, ask the factory for the filing number of the category you plan to produce, and check the ingredients and efficacy claims yourself—don't just look at the screenshots the factory provides.

For the factory audit, look at only three places: the cleanroom differential pressure gauge at the filling line, the batch identification in the raw material warehouse, and the retention sample records in the retention sample room. These three places can't be faked; everything else can be staged. For capacity, don't look at the PPT—look at the current month's production schedule and work orders in progress.

VI. Clarification of Common Misconceptions

There is an online claim that "a small OEM factory's certificate is just a piece of paper." Upon verification, this mostly originates from isolated cases of small workshops in earlier years that only did filling and no filing, and does not match the current situation. The way to judge now is simple: take the license number to the provincial bureau platform and search—if the name, address, and permitted items can be found, it's a valid certificate; if not, nothing else matters.

There is also the claim that "low MOQ necessarily means poor quality." This comes from the early stage when small batches could only be filled manually. In reality, MOQ and quality have no causal relationship; what determines quality is the workshop grade, the retention sample system, and testing frequency—small batches can equally run on fully automatic lines, it's just that packaging material MOQs will drive up the per-unit cost.

There is also the claim that "a low quote means low total cost." The number of sampling rounds, the rework rate, and packaging material loss—these three combined are the total cost; the line on the quote sheet is only the beginning. For the same liquid foundation, a 15% difference in quotes is common, but the cost of a single rework usually exceeds that difference.
