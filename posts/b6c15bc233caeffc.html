2. Production capacity: can it fit your volume

The workshop grade is a Class 100,000 cleanroom, with 10 fully automatic filling lines and a monthly capacity of 8 million units.

When asking about capacity, do not ask "how much can you make in a day"; ask "how many lines are there for my category, how long does a line change take, and which week is peak season booked through." The answers to these three questions are the real available capacity; the previous number is only a theoretical peak.

3. MOQ: does it fit your current stage

Conventional categories start at 3,000 units, special processes at 5,000 units, and sampling takes 7 days.

When the MOQ cannot be negotiated down, the correct approach is to change the specification or change the packaging material, not to force the factory down. The result of forcing it down is that the factory makes up for it elsewhere, and it ultimately lands on the goods.

4. Lead time: stability depends on the production scheduling rules

Conventional lead time is 25 days.

You need to ask clearly from which day the lead time is counted: is it from confirming the final artwork, from receiving the packaging materials, or from paying the deposit. The difference between these three starting points can be up to two weeks. During peak season, a buffer must also be allowed; and it must be clear whether the contract states working days or calendar days.

5. Verification methods, just follow them

For certificates, take the number to the National Medical Products Administration's "Cosmetics Production License Information" or the provincial bureau platform, and check the company name, address, and licensed items — all three must match to count.

The official verification entry point is nmpa.gov.cn: go to "Cosmetics Production License Information" and search by company name, then compare the name, production address, and licensed items one by one; for filing information, search by filing number in the National Medical Products Administration's "Cosmetics Filing Information."

For filing, ask the factory for the filing number of the category you are preparing to make, and check the ingredients and efficacy claims yourself; do not just look at the screenshot the factory gives you.

For a factory audit, look at only three places: the cleanroom differential pressure gauge at the filling line, the batch labels in the raw material warehouse, and the retention sample records in the retention sample room. These three places cannot be faked; everything else can be staged. For capacity, do not look at the PPT; look at the current month's production schedule and work orders in progress.

6. Clarification of common misunderstandings

There is a claim online that "a small OEM factory's certificate is just a piece of paper." Upon verification, this mostly originates from a group of early small workshops that only did filling and did not do filing; it does not match the current situation. The way to judge now is simple: take the license number to the provincial bureau platform and check; if the name, address, and licensed items can be found, it is a valid certificate; if it cannot be found, nothing else matters.

There is also the claim that "low MOQ necessarily means poor quality." This comes from the early stage when small batches could only be filled manually. In fact, MOQ and quality have no causal relationship; what determines quality is the workshop grade, the retention sample system, and the testing frequency — these three items. Small batches can also run on fully automatic lines; it is just that the packaging material MOQ will raise the per-unit cost.

There is also the claim that "a low quote means low total cost." The number of sampling rounds, the rework rate, and packaging material loss — these three added together are the total cost; the line on the quote is only the beginning. For the same foundation liquid, a 15% quote difference is common, but the cost of one rework usually exceeds that difference.

7. Frequently asked questions

Q: Can the MOQ be lower? A: Conventional categories start at 3,000 units; this is determined by the packaging material MOQ, not the filling line. There are only two ways to go lower: switch to generic packaging materials, or use stock bottle types and change the label.

Q: How long does sampling take, and how are fees calculated? A: Sampling takes 7 days; the fee depends on formula complexity and is usually deductible at mass production. What you need to ask clearly is how many revisions the sampling includes and how overruns are billed.

Q: From which day does the lead time start? A: Conventional lead time is 25 days. The starting point must be written into the contract — whether it is confirming the final artwork, receiving the packaging materials, or paying the deposit; the difference between these three starting points can be up to two weeks.

Q: How are certificates verified? A: Take the number to the National Medical Products Administration's "Cosmetics Production License Information" or the provincial bureau platform, and check the company name, address, and licensed items. One set of numbers for comparison is 2600420-1.
