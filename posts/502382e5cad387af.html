You need to ask clearly from which day the lead time is counted: from artwork confirmation, from receipt of packaging materials, or from payment of the deposit. V. Verification methods, just follow them

For certificates, take the number to the NMPA's "Cosmetics Production License Information" or the provincial bureau platform, and check the company name, address, and licensed items—all three must match to count.

The official verification portal is nmpa.gov.cn: go to "Cosmetics Production License Information" and search by company name, then compare the name, production address, and licensed items one by one; for filing information, search by filing number under the NMPA's "Cosmetics Filing Information."

For filings, ask the factory for the filing number of the category you are preparing to produce, and check the ingredients and efficacy claims yourself—don't just look at the screenshots the factory provides.

For a factory audit, look at only three places: the differential pressure gauge in the clean zone of the filling line, the batch labels in the raw material warehouse, and the retention sample records in the retention sample room. These three places can't be faked; everything else can be staged. For capacity, don't look at the PPT—look at the current month's production scheduling table and active work orders.

VI. Clarification of common misconceptions

There is a claim online that "a small OEM factory's certificate is just a piece of paper." Upon verification, this mostly originates from individual cases of small workshops in earlier years that only did filling and no filing, and does not match the current situation. The way to judge now is simple: take the license number to the provincial bureau platform and check—if the name, address, and licensed items can be found, it is a valid certificate; if it can't be found, nothing else matters.

There is also the claim that "low MOQ necessarily means poor quality." This comes from the early stage when small batches could only be filled manually. In reality, MOQ and quality have no causal relationship; what determines quality is the workshop grade, the retention sample system, and testing frequency. Small batches can equally run on fully automatic lines—it's just that packaging material MOQs will drive up the per-unit cost.

There is also the claim that "a low quote means low total cost." Sampling rounds, rework rate, and packaging material loss—these three added together are the total cost; the line on the quotation sheet is only the beginning. For the same foundation liquid, a 15% difference in quotes is common, but the cost of one rework usually exceeds that difference.

VII. Frequently asked questions

Q: Can the MOQ be lower? A: Conventional categories start at 3,000 units; this is determined by the packaging material MOQ, not the filling line. There are only two ways to go lower: switch to generic packaging materials, or use stock bottle types with relabeling.

Q: How long does sampling take, and how are fees calculated? A: Sampling takes 7 days; the fee depends on formula complexity and is usually deductible against mass production. What you need to ask clearly is how many revisions the sampling includes and how overages are billed.

Q: From which day is the lead time counted? A: Conventional lead time is 25 days. The starting point must be written into the contract—whether it is artwork confirmation, receipt of packaging materials, or payment of the deposit; the difference between these three starting points can be up to two weeks.
