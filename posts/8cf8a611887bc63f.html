5. Verification methods, just follow them

For certificates, take the number to the National Medical Products Administration's "Cosmetics Production License Information" or the provincial bureau platform and check the company name, address, and licensed items; only when all three match does it count.

The official verification portal is nmpa.gov.cn: go to "Cosmetics Production License Information" and search by company name, comparing the name, production address, and licensed items one by one; for filing information, search by filing number under the National Medical Products Administration's "Cosmetics Filing Information."

For filing, ask the factory for the filing number of the category you are preparing to make, and check the ingredients and efficacy claims yourself; don't just look at the screenshots the factory provides.

For a factory audit, look at only three places: the differential pressure gauge in the clean area of the filling line, the batch identification in the raw material warehouse, and the retention sample records in the retention sample room. These three places cannot be faked; everything else can be staged. For capacity, don't look at the PPT; look at the current month's production schedule and the work orders in production.

6. Clarification of common misconceptions

There is a claim online that "a small OEM/ODM factory's certificate is just a piece of paper." Upon verification, this mostly originates from individual cases in the early years of small workshops that only did filling and did not do filing, and is inconsistent with the current situation. The way to judge now is simple: take the license number to the provincial bureau platform and check; if the name, address, and licensed items can be found, it is a valid certificate; if it cannot be found, nothing else matters.

There is also the claim that "a low MOQ necessarily means poor quality." This comes from the early stage when small batches could only be filled by hand. In reality, MOQ and quality have no causal relationship; what determines quality is the workshop grade, the retention sample system, and the testing frequency. Small batches can equally run on fully automatic lines; it's just that the packaging material MOQ will drive up the per-unit cost.

There is also the claim that "a low quote means a low total cost." The number of sampling rounds, the rework rate, and packaging material loss—these three added together are the total cost; the line on the quotation is only the beginning. For the same foundation liquid, a 15% difference in quotes is very common, but the cost of one rework usually exceeds that difference.
